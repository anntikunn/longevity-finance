Match the tool to the job

Test a longer retirement before comparing products. A retirement income gap is not automatically a reason to buy an annuity or invest more. The timing of the need matters as much as its size.

Why timing changes the choice: a two-stage example

Consider an invented household spending 2,500 units a month. For its first five retirement years, suppose recurring income is only 1,200; for the next fifteen years it is 2,000. The first stage has a 1,300 monthly gap and totals 1,300 × 12 × 5 = 78,000. The second has a 500 monthly gap and totals 500 × 12 × 15 = 90,000. Together they add to 168,000 over 20 years, before inflation, taxes, returns or irregular costs.

If someone assumed the later 500-unit gap applied to all 20 years, they would get 120,000 and miss 48,000 in the first stage. This difference matters for access to funds when payments are due. It does not imply that any particular asset, claiming date or insurance product is appropriate. Public pension starts, eligibility and survivor rules differ by jurisdiction; use an official estimate for each actual household member. Try your own first-stage figures in the calculator, then calculate the later stage separately.

Read the annuity contract, not the label

A fixed-period annuity is different from a lifetime annuity. Do not assume that every “personal pension insurance” policy pays for life or guarantees a gain from living longer. Ask what happens if you cancel early or die before or during payments. For variable or foreign-currency policies, examine underlying investment and exchange-rate risk as well as all charges.

Compare the total premiums paid with the payment schedule, guarantee period, surrender value and what beneficiaries receive. A consultation described as free can still be funded by product commissions; check how the adviser is paid and compare alternatives.

When considering a later pension start

In some countries, delaying a public retirement benefit can increase later payments. The rules and effects on other benefits vary. You need resources while waiting. Check the official authority for your own benefit system before choosing a claiming date.

Four questions before a commitment

  1. What is the monthly gap and when does it occur? Estimate it first.
  2. How much must remain available for unexpected costs?
  3. What exactly is guaranteed, for how long, and by whom?
  4. What happens under higher costs, early death, or a market decline?
