PLANNING GUIDE

Build a first retirement cash-flow estimate

The same first calculation can be used in different countries. Pension eligibility, tax treatment and available products differ by location. This is a starting arithmetic exercise, not a prediction of how long your money will last.

1. List monthly spending

Start with actual household transactions. Include housing, food, utilities, transport and regular health costs. Adjust expenses linked to work and separate occasional costs such as home repairs and travel. Match your spending and income figures on the same basis: gross or after tax.

Illustration in arbitrary currency units: housing 700 + food 600 + utilities and communications 300 + health and insurance 200 + transport and leisure 700 = 2,500 monthly. This is a made-up budget, not an official household average.

2. Check your own pension estimate

Use the official statement or benefit estimator for the system in which you have accrued rights. For example, the US Social Security Administration provides retirement estimates, while Japan Pension Service offers Nenkin Net. These are examples, not universal eligibility guides. If planning as a household, compare household income with household spending. An estimate is conditional and may change with future work or contributions.

3. Calculate the monthly gap

Monthly spending minus recurring income equals the gap you may need to cover. Enter amounts in one consistent currency; the example values 2,500 and 2,000 are arbitrary currency units. The calculation runs in your browser; inputs are not sent or stored by this site.

Simple arithmetic only. Excludes inflation, investment returns, taxes, changing pension income and one-off costs. A negative gap is shown as no shortfall.

In the example, (2,500 − 2,000) × 12 × 20 = 120,000 currency units. Test several periods rather than assuming a precise date of death. Keep a separate allowance for irregular bills.

Important: This calculator omits inflation, taxes, benefit changes, healthcare shocks and investment losses. Its output is not a recommended savings target or a product recommendation.

Stress-test the first answer

  1. Model years before pension payments start, when income may be lower.
  2. Try higher spending and a longer retirement. Do not assume constant purchasing power.
  3. Consider an early market decline if part of the gap will be funded by selling investments.
  4. Keep near-term cash needs distinct from a long-term income gap.

Next, test 20, 30 and 40-year horizons, then compare ways to address the gap.
